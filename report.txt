Fuel Data Analysis: Discovery Report
1. Executive Summary
The purpose of this analysis was to examine the Fuel Data environment, understand how fuel transactions are associated with vehicles, and identify potential data or business-process issues.
The analysis identified a significant issue relating to vehicle registration changes.
Fuel transactions received from the bank contained vehicle registrations that could not always be matched to the vehicle registrations held in the internal system.
Further investigation showed that when certain vehicle registrations changed, the updated registration was available in the bank data but had not been captured in the internal system.
This indicates that what initially appears to be a data-matching problem may actually be a gap in the business process for maintaining vehicle registration information.
The issue has the potential to affect fuel transaction matching, vehicle-level reporting and the reliability of downstream analytics.

2. Discovery
Finding: Vehicle Registration Mismatch
During analysis of fuel transactions, I identified transactions where the vehicle registration supplied by the bank did not match a registration in the internal vehicle data.
I isolated the unmatched transactions and compared them with the existing vehicle records.

3. Investigation
I investigated the exceptions rather than immediately changing the matching logic.
The analysis indicated the following sequence:
A vehicle exists in the internal system with its original registration.
The vehicle registration changes.
The new registration is captured on the bank side.
The corresponding registration change is not captured internally.
Subsequent fuel transactions arrive using the new registration.
The transaction can no longer be reliably matched to the vehicle using registration alone.
The unmatched data then flows downstream into analytics and reporting.
This suggests that the technical data issue is a symptom of an upstream business-process gap rather than simply a reporting defect.

4. Business Impact
The potential impact extends beyond data quality.
Transaction matching
Fuel transactions associated with changed registrations may fail to match to the correct vehicle.
Reporting accuracy
Vehicle-level fuel consumption, fuel cost and usage reporting could potentially be incomplete or incorrectly attributed.
Operational efficiency
Employees may need to investigate and reconcile unmatched transactions manually.
Decision-making
If management reporting relies on the affected data, decisions could be made using incomplete vehicle-level fuel information.
The business impact should be quantified before prioritising the remediation. For example, I would calculate:
Percentage of fuel transactions that cannot be matched.
Number of unique vehicles affected.
Total fuel value represented by affected transactions.
How long affected registrations remain unmatched.
Whether the issue is increasing or decreasing over time.

5. Root-Cause Hypothesis
Based on the available data, my hypothesis is that there is a gap in the process responsible for maintaining vehicle registration changes.
The bank has visibility of the updated registration, while the internal system continues to hold the historical registration.
I would not consider the root cause confirmed from database analysis alone.
The next step would be to validate the finding with the teams responsible for vehicle administration and fuel-card management and establish:
Who is responsible for initiating a registration change?
Which system is considered the source of truth?
How does the bank receive the updated registration?
Should the same change trigger an update internally?
Is the process manual or automated?
Are there validation or reconciliation controls between the two systems?
The answers would determine whether the appropriate solution is a business-process change, system integration, automated reconciliation, or a combination of these.

6. Recommendation
Immediate
Create an exception report that identifies bank registrations that cannot be matched to active vehicle records internally.
This gives the business visibility of the problem while the underlying process is investigated.
Short term
Reconcile the affected registrations and correct the relevant internal vehicle records once they have been validated by the business owner.
Longer term
Reduce reliance on manual detection.
Depending on the capabilities of the source systems, I would investigate either:
Automatically propagating validated registration changes between systems; or
Implementing a scheduled reconciliation that compares bank vehicle information with the internal vehicle master and alerts the appropriate team when differences are detected.
I would also investigate whether a stable vehicle identifier other than registration can be used for system integration. A vehicle registration is an attribute that can change, so it may not be an appropriate long-term integration key.

7. Proposed Control
A useful automated data-quality control would monitor:
Bank registration → Internal vehicle → Match status
For example:
Matched: Registration exists and vehicle can be identified.
Changed: Vehicle can be identified but registration differs.
Unmatched: No reliable corresponding vehicle can be identified.
Potential duplicate: Registration is associated with more than one vehicle.
These exceptions could be surfaced through a dashboard or operational report instead of waiting for the problem to become visible in downstream reporting.

8. Conclusion
The key discovery from the analysis is not simply that some registrations do not match.
The data indicates a potential gap in the business process for maintaining vehicle registration changes across systems.
Rather than masking the issue by changing the reporting logic, I would validate the hypothesis with the relevant process owners, quantify the financial and operational impact, and then implement the smallest practical solution that addresses the problem at source.
This approach improves both the reliability of the data and the underlying business operation.
